# Creator teardown: 8 dimensions, 40 indicators, and a profile template

Use this when you want a full-dimension benchmark of one creator or one viral video. Fill every item. Mark values you saw or measured as "measured"; values taken from comments, profile pages or open-source repositories carry a source; anything you cannot obtain is written "not verified". Never guess.

## Dimension 1: Account positioning, size and growth
1. Platform matrix and account IDs (short video, long video, code hosting, personal site).
2. Followers, total likes, like-to-follower ratio, recent growth speed (for example per month).
3. Persona and one-line positioning.
4. Main aspect ratio and duration range (vertical 9:16 or horizontal 16:9; fast 2-4 minutes versus deep 8-12 minutes).
5. Posting frequency and output ceiling (daily, weekly, or how many per month through automation).

## Dimension 2: Monetization and funnel
6. Core monetization (sponsorships, membership, paid course, open source leading to paid components, SaaS, platform revenue share).
7. Lead hook (free skill, source code, online library, prompt pack, comment keyword).
8. Where followers land (profile link, repository readme, pinned comment).
9. Pacing of ads and commercial content (which second the product appears, how a price is stated, how disclaimers are written).

## Dimension 3: Topic, title and first 5 seconds
10. Recurring hit topic patterns (ranked reviews, open-source roundups, earnings breakdowns, automation cost tests, tool maps).
11. Title formula (does it always name a specific tool, a specific amount, view count or duration, a counterintuitive conflict).
12. Hook in the first 3-5 seconds (show the big bill first, show the finished result first, throw out an extreme comparison number, zero preamble).
13. Narrative skeleton (summary-detail-summary ranking, three ledgers, timeline, cause breakdown).
14. Comment strategy (inviting sides, pinned follow-up, replying with the tech stack to create second-round sharing).

## Dimension 4: Cover and packaging
15. Cover aspect ratio and composition template (3:4, 4:3, 16:9; portrait size and position).
16. Cover text hierarchy (word limit, weight, color contrast such as white plus yellow, whether text overlaps the edge or the portrait).
17. Visual hammer and brand markers (fixed gesture, tool logo wall, big data numbers, consistent corner badge).

## Dimension 5: Live-action layer (camera, lighting, presenter)
18. How the person appears (full live action, live action plus AI cutout half body, corner circle picture-in-picture, avatar, voice only).
19. Shot size, camera height, negative space (which side is reserved for cards).
20. Lighting measured (key light direction, whether background accent lights cycle hue and with what period in seconds, dark or bright key).
21. Wardrobe and prop restraint (solid clothing so it does not fight the foreground info, fixed set or chair as identity).
22. Performance and gesture density (looking at the lens or a prompter, gesture rhythm, how often the expression changes).

## Dimension 6: Editing rhythm and audio-visual sync
23. Measured cut statistics (average and median shot length at a low scene-change threshold such as 0.04 for subtle visual changes and a higher one such as 0.12 for big scene changes).
24. Local information refresh rate (how often a card, number, underline, flip or zoom appears).
25. Audio to visual delay (how many hundred milliseconds after a keyword is spoken the visual appears; give at least 5 exact examples in seconds).
26. Transitions and insert handoffs (hard cut, fade, bringing the presenter in early under an insert, continuous handoff of component objects).
27. Share and source labeling of b-roll, official sample footage and screen recordings.

## Dimension 7: Motion graphics, captions and technical stack
28. Production tool chain (AI coding assistant plus After Effects, Remotion, pure HTML canvas, consumer editors, DaVinci, screen-recording zoom tools).
29. Cutout method (green screen versus AI matting; hair-edge quality).
30. Color table (measured by sampling: main background, card color, accent, highlight, warning color, as exact hex).
31. Font system and burned-in caption geometry (fonts for each script, glyph height in px at 1080p, vertical position in percent, single or bilingual, box or outline).
32. Full inventory of every pop-up, chart and metaphor animation (no omissions).
33. Automated quality gates (checks for black frames, flicker, overlapping text, frozen slow pushes, duplicate components).

## Dimension 8: Audio engineering and replication plan
34. Voice recording and processing chain (real versus cloned voice; speed-up such as 1.1x; gain, noise reduction, whether natural filler words are kept).
35. Breath-gap editing density (number of silence gaps, from a silence detector).
36. Background music style, tempo and ducking (continuous bed or sectioned; does it step aside on original-voice sections).
37. Sound effect type, density and restraint rules (flips, pop-ups, drips, a limited number per episode).
38. Replaceability score from 1 to 5: with the real person removed, how much of the visual and content skeleton keeps its punch.
39. Three replication tiers (high-fidelity real presenter; digital human or avatar stand-in; real voice without a face, fully automated).
40. Single standout techniques worth keeping in a reusable library of moves (if you keep one), marked as "automate", "turn into a skill", or "reference only".

## Standard procedure for each new creator or video

1. Survey the account and public sources first: profile page, public channels, and whether the creator also publishes on other platforms or open-sources workflows, skills or component libraries (read source before guessing).
2. Silent pull and measurement: metadata, per-second contact sheets (even and odd seconds), cut-point statistics at three thresholds, background light hue curve, loudness and silence statistics, subtitle fetch. Tools needed: ffmpeg, ffprobe and an imaging library.
3. Manual visual review: read every sheet, high-resolution frames, 0.2 s motion strips, listen to audio excerpts; no omissions or misjudgments.
4. File it twice: a per-creator folder (overview file plus a per-video teardown report) and a cross-creator index; distill each high-value new trick into one short "building block" entry (if you keep a reusable library of moves, add it there).
5. Report: plain-language core conclusion first (their three strongest moves, how they differ from creators you studied before, how you can use them); details and parameters go into files.

## Creator profile template

Copy this into a per-creator file and fill every item, noting the source for each (profile screenshot, measured, open-source repository, comments, inference, not verified).

- Created / updated: YYYY-MM-DD
- Style label (example: dark studio talking head plus AI-cutout inserts plus motion graphics; or: fully automated component-driven infographics)
- Replaceability index, 1 to 5 stars, with a one-sentence reason

### 1. Account and data
Platforms and IDs; size snapshot with date; core positioning; main aspect ratio and length; output and cadence.

### 2. Monetization and funnel
Core paid product; lead hook; landing point; how sponsorships are presented.

### 3. Topic, title and cover system
Top 5 recurring topics; title formula; cover rules (ratio, text hierarchy, colors, visual hammer); first-5-seconds pattern.

### 4. Live action, lighting, presenter
Presenter form; camera, lighting and background motion; cutout and layering method.

### 5. Editing rhythm, motion stack, quality engineering
Measured cuts and rhythm; production tool stack (verified from the creator's own words or source); signature motion components; colors, fonts and caption hard specs; quality safeguards.

### 6. Audio
Voice chain (speed, gain, breath gaps, keeping verbal tics); background music and sound effect strategy.

### 7. Replication assessment
The 3 strongest things to take; 2 pitfalls not to copy; IDs of building blocks already filed.
